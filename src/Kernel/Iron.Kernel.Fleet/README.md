# Iron.Kernel.Fleet

Kernel module (ADR-0029). Schema `kernel_fleet`. Provisioning (TF-002, TF-210; ADR-0018). The control plane API lists the module for jobs; the fleet worker lists it and runs them.

| Folder | Holds |
|---|---|
| `Contracts/` | `IProvisioningJobs`, `IJobHandovers` (a job's link, once, to whoever asked), `ProvisioningJobInfo` with its steps, `JobKind` (Provision, Deprovision, Update, Recover, Impersonate, BreakGlassOpen, BreakGlassClose), `JobRequest` (whom a job is about, why, who asked, whether a recovery also resets the administrator's second factors, the command's `Environment` and to whom its handover goes, `HandOverTo`), `ProvisioningStatus`, `FleetPermissions`, `FleetErrors` |
| `Data/` | `FleetDbContext`, `ProvisioningJob` (who asked, by name and id, when its handover was given, a recovery job's subject, reason and `reset_factors`, and an operator command's `environment` as jsonb), `ProvisioningStep`, the design-time factory, generated `Migrations/` |
| `Services/` | `ProvisioningRunner` (the steps in order, recorded, resumable), `ProvisioningWorker` (the loop), `ProvisioningJobs`, `IUnitRuntime` (Compose in T14b), `FleetOptions`, `SizeClasses` |
| `Services/Steps/` | reserve, database, storage, secrets, migrate, seed, unit, verify, validation-pack; the secrets step writes the unit's policy on every run (the unit secret and `tenants/<slug>/entitlements`, ADR-0034, read; its single sign-on providers' client secrets at `tenants/<slug>/sso/*`, managed, ADR-0048) and the tenant's entitlements through Tenancy's `ITenantEntitlements`; remove (de-provisioning); recover, the one step of a recovery job; impersonate, break-glass-open and break-glass-close, the one step of each operator command (`OperatorCommandStep`) |

A link from the operator's path (ADR-0046) never travels through a job's output, a log line or a trace. The seed and recover-admin steps give their command the job's one-time place, `tenants/<slug>/handover/<job id>`, emptied first, as `Handover__Path`. They also give `Handover__Token`, which lives 10 minutes under the policy `tenant-<slug>-handover` and may write the tenant's handover places and nothing else. The command writes the link there once, for its lifetime, and says so. `JobHandovers` gives it once, after the job completed, to the principal who asked (`requested_by_id`), deletes the place and stamps `handed_over_at`.

A recovery job (TF-017, ADR-0046) runs the image's `recover-admin` command in an active tenant's unit, as the owner role like the seed, with the administrator, the operator and the reason in `Recovery__LoginId`, `Recovery__Operator` and `Recovery__Reason`, `Recovery__ResetFactors=true` when the operator asked for the administrator's second factors to be reset too (ADR-0047), and its handover place; the step's output then says the factors were reset. A refusal in the unit (not a tenant administrator, not active) fails the step with the command's reason. The tenant's status never changes, and a job with a request is never reopened: a new request is a new job.

An operator command (TF-025, TF-026, ADR-0049) runs the image's `impersonate`, `break-glass-open` or `break-glass-close` in an active tenant's unit, as the owner role, with the request's `Environment` (`Impersonation__*` or `BreakGlass__*`, never a secret: the job keeps it) and the job's handover place. The command hands over a sign-in link, or the break-glass review, and says so; the step reports it and holds neither. A link goes to `HandOverTo`, or to who asked; the review is collected once by the control plane (`IJobHandovers.CollectAsync`), never by a person. The secrets step's unit policy may also read `platform/documents`.

The audit chain (TF-061, ADR-0050). The database step gives each tenant's database a verifier role, `<tenant>_audit`, that can read the chain's tables (`IVerifierReadable`) and nothing else, its password at `tenants/<slug>/database` (key `audit_password`), which the unit's policy does not reach. The secrets step makes the unit's own control-plane client through Identity's `IServiceAccounts` (the `unit-client` role), binds it to the tenant through Tenancy's `ITenantAuditChains`, and puts its id, secret and the control plane's address for units in the unit secret (`ControlPlane:Address`, `ClientId`, `ClientSecret`). `make update-unit` does both for a unit made before T6. `AuditVerificationWorker` asks the control plane every minute what is due and runs `AuditChainVerifier`: the database's part of the chain with the verifier role on the fleet's own route to the server, the archived part from the tenant's container with the fleet's account (`ArchivedChainReader`), checked by `AuditChainCheck` against the anchors and the segments that accepted restores began (each anchor counts in the segment that covers its position, and each segment's first event must link to its restore point), and the record sent back. A tenant it cannot reach is tried again an hour later.

The validation pack (TF-218, ADR-0052). The ninth step of a provisioning and of an update, `validation-pack`, asks the unit's API image its version (the image's `version` command, which builds no host) and copies the pack of exactly that version from the platform's releases container (`<version>/validation-pack-<version>.zip`, `ReleasePacks`) into the tenant's container at `validation/<version>/validation-pack-<version>.zip`, where the unit's Validation page reads it. A pack not published yet never fails the step, since an update must heal a unit: the step says so, and the unit says the pack is missing. The fleet's image publishes a pack to the releases container with `publish-validation-pack <version> <file>` (`ReleasePackCommand`, a unit command the fleet host runs as it runs the database commands): the release's pipeline for a deployment, `make validation-pack` on the laptop for the development pack, which the next `make update-unit` places.

Configuration (`Fleet` section): `Database:AdminConnectionString`, `Database:UnitHost`, `Database:UnitPort`; `Storage:AccountConnectionString`, `Storage:UnitBlobEndpoint`, `Storage:SasValidDays`, `Storage:ReleasesContainer` (`releases`); `Secrets:UnitAddress`, `Secrets:UnitTokenValidDays`; `Units:ImageTag`, `Units:OtlpEndpoint`, `Units:ReadyTimeoutSeconds`; `ControlPlane:UnitAddress` (the control plane as units reach it, `http://control-api:8080` in Compose); `Audit:PollSeconds` (60); `PollSeconds`. The fleet's own store comes from the `Secrets` section as for any unit.

Model change: edit the entity, then `make ef-add MODULE=Fleet NAME=<WhatChanged>` and commit `Data/Migrations`.

The control plane's own database and chain (ADR-0053): the image's `prepare-control` gives the control plane the split a unit has. As the superuser it makes `control_owner` (owns the `control` database, migrates and seeds), `control_app` (what the control plane's API and the fleet run with) and `control_audit` (reads the chain's tables and nothing else), moves a database made before this to the owner, makes the container `control-audit-anchors` with a SAS that may read, list and create blobs and never write over or delete one, and writes `control/database` (the passwords), `control/unit` (the app role's connection, `ControlAudit:AnchorsSasUrl`) and `control-migrate/unit` (the owner's connection, `Database:AppRole`, `Database:AuditReaderRole`). `BlobAuditAnchorStore` keeps one blob per anchor there. `ControlAuditVerifier` checks the control plane's chain with `control_audit` against those anchors and records the result under `control`, beside the tenants'; `ControlAuditVerificationWorker` runs it daily in the fleet host, in full monthly, and the image's `verify-control-audit [full]` runs it by hand (exit 0 when it passed, 2 when not).

Keys and passwords (TF-073, TF-075, ADR-0054). The secrets step makes the tenant's three keys in the key service (`tenant-<slug>-data`, `-index`, `-signing`, through `IKeyAdministration`), records them and the database password's version in the control plane (`ITenantKeys`), gives the unit's policy the use of its own keys and nothing more (`SecretsStep.UnitKeyGrants`), and writes `Keys:Prefix` into the unit secret instead of a signing key; a unit secret made before T9b loses its factor key to the fleet secret (`legacy_factor_key`), which the seed step hands once to the image's seed as `Identity__LegacyFactorKey` and removes after it succeeded. `KeyRotationWorker` runs `KeyRotationRunner` every `Fleet:Keys:PollSeconds` (5): it asks for the rotations the schedule says are due, rotates each one asked for in the key service, or, for the database password, through `DatabasePasswordRotation` (the store first, the database secret and the unit's connection string, then `ALTER ROLE`; the version beside it, `app_password_version`), and retires the old version of every key whose unit reported every value moved, or a signing key 30 minutes after its rotation. A failure is recorded with its error and raises the alert.

The control plane's API and the fleet each have their own secret and token (ADR-0054): `prepare-control` reads the administrative connections from `fleet/unit`, which `make secret-control` writes first, and writes `control/unit` (the API), `fleet/unit` (the fleet, with `Secrets:TokenRole=tenant-units`) and `control-migrate/unit`; `ControlPlaneAccess` makes the control plane's keys, the two policies (`ApiRules`, `FleetRules`), the token role, and the two tokens, kept at `control-tokens/unit`, where only the root token reads them, and kept as long as they open the store.
