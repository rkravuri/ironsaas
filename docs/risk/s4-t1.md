The task touched these requirements (classes from `docs/trace/risk.md`):

| Requirement | Class | How it was touched |
|---|---|---|
| TF-085 Transactional outbox | critical (Data platform) | Built: the outbox written in the change's unit of work, beside its audit event, and the worker's dispatcher |
| TF-037 Delegation of duties | critical (Authorization) | Deactivating a person now ends their running delegations, through the event `identity.person.deactivated`; an ended delegation publishes `authz.delegation.ended` |
| TF-022 Session management console | critical (Sessions, tokens and service accounts) | A delegate's sessions stop acting for the delegator as soon as the delegation ends, audited, rather than at their next request |

The change is classed **critical**.

**What could go wrong.** An event could exist without its change (a rollback that leaves it), or a committed change lose its event (published outside the transaction, or a crash between commit and dispatch). A subscriber could handle an event twice, by two workers or a replay, and double its effect; or handle a record's events out of order. One failing subscriber could hold up the others, or its own other records, or retry forever without anyone knowing. An event could carry personal data or a copy of a record into a store with a different retention. A replay could run without a reason or a recent sign-in, or unaudited, or by someone without the permission. A unit without a suite could hold or deliver the suite's events. Deactivating a person could fail to end their delegations, end someone else's, or leave a delegate acting for them. A host could fail to start because a module it runs now needs the Events module. The operator's search could print an event's data in a job's output. When a suite is removed, its subscribers' waiting deliveries could raise alerts and dead letters for work nobody owes, be lost, or fail to resume when the suite returns; and the hold could hide a Foundation subscriber that disappeared by mistake.

**What proves it does not.** The publisher throws outside a unit of work, and a rollback leaves no outbox row (`OutboxTests`); an event committed while no worker runs is delivered by the next worker started after it (`OutboxTests`, the crash and restart); a real worker hands 20 events on within 10 seconds (TF-085 AC1), and on the stack the smoke's `events` step sees a sample record's event handled by its subscriber within the same bound. Two deliveries of one inbox row at once, and a later redelivery, run the handler once (TF-085 AC3, TF-111 AC2, `DispatchTests`); events of one record reach a subscriber in position order while others run in parallel; a failing subscriber is retried with growing waits, then dead-lettered with its error and audited, while the event's other subscriber and the failing one's other records go on, and a dead letter holds back nothing after it (TF-085 AC2, TF-111 AC1, `DispatchTests`). The declarations refuse a field that is not an identifier, code, number, flag, time or day, free text, a nested record, and `[PersonalData]` on anything but an identifier, and data that fails its declaration is refused before anything is written (TF-110 AC2, `EventCatalogueTests`); the catalogue lists each event with its fields and subscribers, and both versions with the old one's deprecation day (TF-110 AC1, AC3), and the host test keeps the file equal to the code. A replay of a dead letter runs the handler again and updates its status (TF-112 AC1), a replay of handled events changes nothing, needs a reason and a known subscriber, and each replay is audited (`ReplayAndArchiveTests`); the endpoints refuse a person without the permission, and the replay asks for a recent sign-in (TF-112 AC3, `EventsEndpointTests`); an event past the retention is archived to the tenant's container and reported as archived (TF-112 AC2). The unit's `events` command lists dead letters without their data and replays in the operator's name (`ReplayAndArchiveTests`), and the fleet's job carries what the control plane asked, with a reason in both trails (`OperatorAccessTests`). A unit without the eProcurement Suite declares and writes none of its events (`SuiteInstallationTests`), and no subscription of any unit composition is unmatched (`HostTests`). Deactivating a delegator ends the delegation through its event, and the delegate stops acting at once, audited (`ActingForTests`). Every host's services are built and validated in the host tests, the control plane's and the fleet's among them. The browser test follows a sample record's event to the console's page on the stack and replays it to the subscriber that handled it: nothing changes and the replay is on the trail. At the review's request, a removed suite's waiting delivery is held at the first pass of the unit without the suite, with no attempt, no error, no audit event and no dead letter, still held past every retry, keeps its event from the archive, and is handled once when the suite returns; a Foundation subscriber the unit no longer holds is still a dead letter, audited (`SuiteHoldTests`); the console shows "Held: suite removed" with the time and offers no replay to it (`EventsPage.test.tsx`, and the scan in both schemes).
