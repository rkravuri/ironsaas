# Risk: S4 T2, background jobs, the job console, fairness, retries and dead letters

The task touched these requirements (classes from `docs/trace/risk.md`):

| Requirement | Class | How it was touched |
|---|---|---|
| TF-003 Tenant isolation | critical (Tenancy and control plane) | A job's database connection and its telemetry are its tenant's: proven, and its exception row removed |
| TF-007 and TF-210 Resource limits and size classes | critical (Tenancy and control plane) | A unit's app role gains a connection limit on the database server by size class, and each process's pool a third of it |
| TF-002 Automated tenant provisioning | critical (Tenancy and control plane) | A new fleet job, `jobs`, runs the unit's command for the operator's list and actions; the database step sets the connection limit at every provisioning and update |

The change is classed **critical**.

**What could go wrong.** Two workers could run one occurrence twice, or none; a run could run twice after a retry or
a recovered lease and double its effect. A run could hang without end, or a failing job retry forever without anyone
knowing, or a job silently stop running. The loops' move could change their behaviour: the chain sealed later or not at
all, events handed on more slowly, retention deleting more or less, the caches refreshed less often or in only one
process, so a kill switch or a plan change would not reach every process. A tenant's bulk work could starve another
tenant's, or its own interactive work. A suspended tenant's jobs could keep running. A job's queries or spans could
reach another tenant, or a request's trace be lost. A person without the permission could pause housekeeping, or
anyone pause it without a reason or a recent sign-in, or unaudited; the operator's actions could bypass the unit's
trail. A suite's jobs could run where the suite is not, or be lost or dead-lettered when it is removed. The connection
limit could refuse a busy unit's own connections, since a pool larger than the limit fails rather than waits.

**What proves it does not.** Two runners on one database over six minutes of a minute's job run six occurrences, each
once (TF-120 AC1); a run past its timeout is cancelled, logged and retried, and a retried run has the effect of one
(TF-120 AC2, AC3); a worker that stops mid-run leaves its run to another once its lease passes (`RunnerTests`). A
transient failure waits a growing, jittered delay, then after its last attempt is a dead letter with its error,
audited, and the dead letter and a missed schedule each raise an alert, as ten dead letters in an hour do (TF-123 AC1
to AC3, `RunnerTests`, `RuleEvaluationTests`). Interactive work is claimed before bulk, an undefined class is refused
where the job is declared, a suspended tenant's due jobs are recorded as skipped and nothing runs (TF-122 AC2 to AC4).
A tenant that holds every connection its size allows is refused more on PostgreSQL while another tenant connects, and
each process's pool is a third of the limit, so a full pool waits (TF-122 AC1, `ProvisioningTests`); the laptop's
measurement with two units is below. A new tenant's seed makes every declared job active and scheduled, the worker's
loops among them (TF-124 AC1), and the host test keeps the catalogue's jobs equal to the code. The loops keep their
tests, which now run the job: the outbox delivers within TF-085's bound (`OutboxTests`), the archive, the conflicts
report and the reviews' escalation behave as before, the refreshers learn a change within 30 seconds without a restart
in every process (`FlagRefreshTests`, `RefreshTests`, `SettingsStoreTests`), and a configured interval keeps its old
bound. A run carries its tenant in its span and links to the request that started it, and runs on its unit's connection
(TF-003 AC2, TF-130 AC3, `ScopeTests`). The console shows a failed run's error and log (TF-121 AC1), a paused job skips
its runs until resumed with its state visible, both audited (AC2), and refuses a person without the permission (AC3);
each action needs a reason and a recent sign-in (`ConsoleTests`, the page's Vitest tests, the scan in both schemes).
The unit's `jobs` command pauses in the operator's name and refuses without a reason, and the fleet's job carries what
the control plane asked, with the reason in both trails (`ConsoleTests`, `OperatorAccessTests`). A suite's job is
declared only where the suite runs, its queued runs are held when it is removed and resumed when it returns
(`RunnerTests`). The smoke starts a job through the console and finds its run; the browser test does the same from the
page and finds the start on the trail.

**Results.** make verify passed: 1317 .NET test cases (1024 methods), 426 Vitest, 164 scans (79 scan tests), 97
Python; the gate with nothing failing but the one approved exception (498 licences); coverage 90.26% and 85.21% against
floors of 90 and 85; the matrix: TF-120 3 of 3 criteria with a test (AC1 1 test, AC2 1, AC3 1), TF-121 3 of 3 (4, 3,
1), TF-122 4 of 4 (1 each), TF-123 3 of 3 (2, 2, 2), TF-124 1 of 3 (AC1 1; AC2 waits for T3's orphan purge and AC3 for
the first Azure deployment's backup verification), TF-003 AC2 and TF-130 AC3 each with a test and out of the
exceptions; 38 tests name ADR-0061; 96 of 500 requirements and 325 of 1748 criteria with a test; S1, S2, S3 and V done
slices with 323 criteria, 294 with a test, 29 exceptions, none missing; 7 untraced. On the laptop, on the branch's images: the control plane, acme, bravo and echo updated, each app role at most 30 connections; `make jobs TENANT=echo` listed its 18 jobs, every maintenance job having run; smoke 28 of 28 on echo, its `jobs` step's run picked up 196 ms after it was queued; browser tests 46 of 46 on echo. The fairness measurement: while echo ran eight saturating runs at a time with 1,636 queued, at its limit of half a processor and holding 12 connections, acme's runs started by hand waited 1.3 seconds from queued to started and its scheduled runs 0.4 (565 ms the longest). Two workers on echo for five minutes ran each of 31 occurrences once; a restarted worker's runs were taken by the new process 16 to 17 seconds after the stop, as it started. The laptop found three faults the tests had not, each fixed with a test before these results: a term job's lag carried from run to run (17 seconds on acme, 78 for `keys.reencrypt`), a stopping worker's runs waited for their lease (two minutes without sealing or dispatch after an update), and the operator's answer lost its tail to the fleet's 2,000-character report.

**What is left.** Notification delivery (T4), webhook delivery (T9), the file quarantine and orphan purge (T3, TF-124
AC2), metering (S6, with billing) and backup verification (the first Azure deployment, TF-124 AC3) will each be a job
declared as these are. The control plane's loops stay hosted services until S6's operator console. A job that calls
outside the unit must be idempotent there too.
