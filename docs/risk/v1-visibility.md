# Risk: V1, content visibility and visibility tags

The task touched these requirements (classes from `docs/trace/risk.md`):

| Requirement | Class | How it was touched |
|---|---|---|
| TF-241 Content visibility and visibility tags | critical (Visibility and access) | Built: the tags, people's and records' tags, the filter in the record policy's query, rules 5 and 6, the explanation's check, reviews of tags |
| TF-035 Record-level policies | critical (Authorization) | The policy engine gains the tag filter after the scope rule and the module's policy, for lists and reads by id, and the action `tag` |
| TF-038 Access reviews | critical (Authorization) | Items list the tags each person holds; a campaign can review tag holders, a revoke taking the tag away |
| TF-039 Effective access explanation | critical (Authorization) | The explanation gains its sixth check, the visibility tags |
| TF-062 Change history (field level) | critical (Audit and compliance) | A record's tags are a field of its history; a person's tags change on their record's history |
| TF-210 Per-tenant deployment units | critical (Tenancy and control plane) | A unit secret may carry `Samples:Enabled` for a tenant marked for testing, and the API host loads the sample module only then |
| TF-218 Validation support for regulated tenants | critical (Regulated-industry controls) | The installation record checks a marked unit's seeds against the image's seeds with the sample module |

The change is classed **critical**.

**What could go wrong.** A record could be shown to someone outside its tags: in a list, in a list's total, by id, through a share or the working unit, or because a tag was retired. A person could see more than their roles allow because the filter widened something. A person could put a tag they do not hold on a record, or take one off, to move it into their own sight or out of others'; an administrator could give someone a tag they do not hold. A person acting for someone could be filtered by the wrong person's tags. A tag change could go unaudited. The explanation could disagree with the real call. The sample module could reach a customer's unit, or its permissions a customer's roles. The storage chosen could make every list slow. A product's record type could map its tags away from the `visibility_tags` column, so its records could not be filtered by them.

**What proves it does not.** The filter is one more condition joined with AND after the scope rule and the policy, in the same query (`PolicyEvaluation.ReadableAsync`); the data tests prove, on PostgreSQL, that a reader of one tag lists a record of that tag and an untagged one and not one of another tag (TF-241 AC1, AC3), that a record of another tag by id is not found and the explanation compares the tags (AC2), that a reader of no tags sees all (AC4), that of 100 records the total and the pages count the 40 the reader sees (AC6), that a share and the working unit are narrowed and never widened, and that a retired tag still filters, can be taken off and not put on (`VisibilityTagsTests`). Rule 5 refuses, naming the tag, a tag the person does not hold, put on or taken off (AC5); the escalation rule does the same for people's tags, a retired tag is given to nobody, and a person acting for someone is filtered by the delegator's tags (`VisibilityTagTests`). A tag change on a record is in its field history and on a person in their record's history, with before and after, and in their security activity (AC7, `VisibilityTagsTests`, `VisibilityTagTests`, `SecurityActivityTests`); the next review lists the person's tags, and a review of tag holders revokes a holding at once (`TagReviewTests`). An order takes its supplier's tags when created and keeps them (AC8). The explanation's status equals the real call's over a matrix with crates of another tag and of a shared one (`ExplainAccessTests`). The sample module is loaded only where the unit secret switches it on, never named by the catalogue, and the fleet writes the switch only for a marked tenant (`HostTests`, `ProvisioningTests`); the installation record checks a marked unit against the seeds with its permissions and every other against those without (`installation_record_test.py`). The measurement chose the column over a kernel table: a list page of 50 in 0.14 to 0.38 ms against 19 to 47 ms, its total in 15 to 22 ms against 40 to 72 ms (ADR-0057). The model check refuses a business record whose table has no `text[]` column `visibility_tags`, renamed or converted (`VisibilityTagsTests`), and every module's context passes it in the build (`ModuleDbContextTests`), added at the design session's review.

**Results.** make verify passed on its third run (the first failed T9b's password rotation test, whose count began before the pool's clear and now begins at it; the second a Vitest test that read the review's old words): 1236 .NET test cases (943 methods), 409 Vitest, 154 scans, 93 Python; the gate with nothing failing; coverage 90.46% and 85.43% against floors of 90 and 85; the matrix: TF-241 8 of 8 criteria with a test (AC1 2, AC2 5, AC3 2, AC4 1, AC5 2, AC6 1, AC7 6, AC8 1), S1 to S3 done with 31 exceptions and none missing. On the laptop, on the branch's images: acme, bravo and echo updated, echo marked for sample records; smoke 25 of 25 on echo, its `visibility-tags` step listing a record tagged SMOKE-A and SMOKE-B and an untagged one to a reader of SMOKE-A, and answering 404 for one tagged SMOKE-B only; browser tests 43 of 43 on echo, a tag defined in the console and put on a record whose History shows it, and the explanation's six checks; the `smoke` tenant marked and updated, and the smoke 25 of 25 on it. After the review, with the model check's refusal (e9c576c), make verify passed again on its first run: 1237 .NET test cases (944 methods), 409 Vitest, 154 scans, 93 Python; the gate with nothing failing; coverage 90.46% and 85.47%; the matrix: TF-241 8 of 8 criteria with a test as before, and 32 tests name ADR-0057. The laptop's images were not built again: the change is a check the build's tests run, and the product's behaviour is unchanged.

**What is left.** Approvals, tasks and notifications that respect the tags are TF-243, for the workflow and notification slices. A product that needs records found by a tag adds a GIN index on its column then. A person who holds no tags sees every record their roles allow, by the owner's rule 3; the reviews list each person's tags so a reviewer sees it.
