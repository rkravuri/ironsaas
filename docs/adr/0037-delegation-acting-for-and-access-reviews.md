# ADR-0037: Delegation of duties, acting for the delegator, and access reviews

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Decided by:** Product owner and architect

## Context

TF-037 asks that a person hand their duties to someone else for a period, with a reason, or that an administrator do it for them; that overlapping, circular and onward delegations are refused; and that every action of the delegate names both people. TF-038 asks for access review campaigns: the principals and roles of each unit listed for its reviewers, who decide only their units; a revoke that ends the assignment at once; a non-reviewer refused; overdue reviews reported and escalated.

The grants of a principal come from one place, `AccessGrants` (ADR-0030, ADR-0031): the evaluator, the row filter of every list, the rule that nobody grants more than they hold, and `/me` all read it. The audit event (ADR-0013) has had an `impersonator_id` column since S1, kept for the operator's support access. A manager, an approval limit and a message are business or later matters (ADR-0029, S4, S5).

## Decision

1. **Duties are declared by modules.** `IModule.Duties` lists a module's duties, each a code, a name in words, a description and the permissions it covers. The kernel declares `all`, which is every permission the person holds; no module declares it, and a code declared twice stops the host at start. Identity declares `identity.user_administration`: reading and managing users, their invitations, their role assignments, and the roles and units an assignment names. Approval, receiving and evaluation are declared by the slices that build them. The catalogue lists the duties with their permissions.
2. **A delegation** is a delegator, a delegate, one or more duties, a period of two instants, a reason, who created it, and an end when someone ends it early; the row stays, so the history of who could act for whom is whole. Its status is scheduled, active, ended early or over, from those facts and the clock. A person delegates their own duties without a permission; an administrator delegates for someone with `authz.delegations.manage` at that person's home unit (tenant-wide for a person without one). The delegator, the delegate or such an administrator ends one early. Creating and ending are audited. The shell sends days in the person's own time zone: from the start of the first day, or from now when it is today, to the end of the last day.
3. **What is refused, each with its own code.** To oneself (`authz.delegation.self`). A service account on either side (`service_account`). The same delegator and a shared duty in periods that overlap (`overlap`); `all` shares every duty. A cycle of any length among delegations whose periods overlap the new one (`cycle`), found by walking the delegate's onward delegations. Onward delegation (`onward`): creating one while acting for someone, or delegating a duty the delegator holds by a delegation to them in an overlapping period. A period that has ended or ends before it starts (`period`), and a duty nobody declared (`duty_unknown`). Creation takes an advisory lock, so two requests cannot both pass the checks.
4. **Acting for someone is explicit, and never a union.** The session carries `acting_for_id`, set by `POST /api/v1/me/acting-for` and cleared by `DELETE`, only while a delegation from that person to the asker is active, and only for a shell session: a token never acts (`identity.acting.token`). While it is set, `AccessGrants` answers for the session's principal the delegator's own grants at the delegator's own scopes, keeping only the permissions of the delegated duties (`all` keeps every one). The person's own rights are not in use. So every check, every list's rows, the no-escalation rule and `/me` follow without code of their own, and the delegate holds exactly what the delegator holds now: a role the delegator loses is gone for the delegate at once in that process, and within the grants cache's 60 seconds elsewhere (ADR-0012). Several active delegations from the same person count together, until the latest end.
5. **The first request after the delegation ends clears the acting.** Whether it expired or was ended early, the request runs as the person themselves, the session forgets the delegator, `identity.acting_ended` is audited, and the answer carries the header `Iron-Acting-Ended` with the delegator's id, which the shell turns into a sentence.
6. **Every audit event names both people.** `kernel_audit.events` gains `on_behalf_of_id`, which the audit writer fills from the request's principal while it acts. `impersonator_id` keeps its own meaning, the operator's support access, which is a different act by a different kind of person. The audit list answers both names and filters by `on_behalf_of_id`. `/me` answers `acting_for` (whom, which duties, until when) and the active delegations both ways.
7. **A directory for choosing a delegate.** Anyone may delegate, including people who may not list users, so `GET /api/v1/directory?search=` answers, for two letters or more of a name or login, at most 20 active people with their name and home unit, and nothing more.
8. **A campaign is a snapshot.** It has a name, its units (each with or without the units below it), a due date, an owner (the person who starts it) and a status, open or closed. Starting it, with `authz.reviews.manage` tenant-wide, writes one item per assignment in its units: the principal and their name, the role's code and name, the scope, and the unit with its path by id and by name, so the reviewer reads what was reviewed after a rename or a removal. A tenant-wide assignment sits at the root and is in a campaign only when the campaign covers the root. A record assignment belongs to its record and is not in a unit's review.
9. **Reviewers are whoever holds `authz.reviews.decide` at an item's unit.** No owner field is added to the tree. The item list uses the same row filter as every unit-scoped list, and a decision is checked at the item's unit, so a reviewer sees and decides the items of their units only. The campaign list and record are for managers and reviewers; anyone else is refused with an audited denial. Managers see the whole campaign's progress, reviewers that of their units.
10. **A decision is confirm or revoke, written once.** It has an optional comment and is append-only, one per item, under a lock per campaign. A revoke removes the assignment through the assignments' own removal path in the transaction that records the decision; the reviewer's right is `authz.reviews.decide`, so `authz.assignments.manage` is not asked, and everything else holds, the last-administrator guard included. When the guard applies, nothing is recorded and the answer is its own error. A revoke of an assignment gone already is recorded with nothing removed. Confirm-all takes a page of at most 200 items and leaves those someone decided meanwhile. Closing, with `authz.reviews.manage`, ends the deciding; open items stay open in the record. Progress counts decided, open and overdue (open after the due date).
11. **Escalation is an extension point.** `IReviewEscalation` in Authorization's Contracts names who is told about late reviewers. The kernel names the campaign's owner; a business module that knows managers replaces it. The unit's worker runs a daily pass, a minute after it starts and then every 24 hours, which escalates every unit of an open campaign 7 days past its due date with items still open, once per campaign and unit: a row with the reviewers and the targets, and the audit event `authz.review_escalated`, in one transaction. Sending the message is S4; until then the event and the campaign's Units tab are the report.
12. **The shell.** The header's "Acting for" control appears only while the person holds an active delegation, or still acts with one (ADR-0033). A calm band under the header says whose rights, which duties, until when, and how to stop, and later that the delegation ended. Console Delegations is open to everyone, so the console's navigation and its opener show it to everyone. Access reviews is for holders of `authz.reviews.decide`.

## Options considered

- The delegate holding their own rights and the delegator's together. Rejected: an audit could not say in whose name an act was done, and a person could combine two people's rights into something neither holds.
- Acting through a second session or a token for the delegator. Rejected: two sessions to keep apart, and a token that speaks for a person is what TF-037 rules out.
- Writing the delegator into `impersonator_id`. Rejected: an operator's support access and a colleague's delegation are different acts, and a report must tell them apart.
- Refusing onward delegation only while acting. Rejected: a person could hand on a duty they hold only by delegation, outside the period they hold it for; the chain would blur who answers for it.
- An owner field on the unit for reviewers. Rejected by the section: the permission at the unit already says who may decide.
- The escalation pass in the fleet worker. Rejected: the campaigns are in the unit's database, which the fleet does not read.
- Reviewing record assignments with the unit's. Deferred: a record's people are its own question, decided where the record is.

## Consequences

- Easier: a module declares a duty in a few lines, and acting for someone works on its endpoints and lists without code of its own.
- The directory shows the names and home units of active people to everyone signed in. Revisit if a tenant needs people hidden from each other.
- A reviewer may decide an item about their own role. Whether that is refused is an open question in STATUS.
- The shell starts a campaign over one unit with or without its children; the API takes several.
- Later: TF-037 AC1 (a workflow assigns the delegate too) is S5; the escalation message is S4; record assignments in reviews.
