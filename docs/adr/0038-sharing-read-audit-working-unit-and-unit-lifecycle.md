# ADR-0038: Record sharing, the cross-unit read audit, the working unit, and the unit lifecycle

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Decided by:** Product owner and architect

## Context

TF-222 asks that access across units be explicit: roles scoped to several units or to a parent with its children, a consolidated view for group functions, records shared with listed units, an audit of every read of another unit's private records, and a switcher in the shell whose choice the defaults follow. TF-223 asks that units be created with a readiness checklist that products supply, moved, merged with the records reassigned and audited, and deactivated.

What a reader may see is decided in one place: the scope rule for a record type's read permission, joined with the module's policy (ADR-0030, ADR-0032). A business record has an owning unit (`BusinessRecord`, ADR-0008). Settings resolve from a unit up to the tenant (ADR-0036). Who acts is the session's principal, or the delegator while acting (ADR-0037). The data library references no module, so what it needs of the tree it declares and Authorization implements (`IRecordAccess`, `IOrganisationUnitCheck`).

## Decision

1. **A business record has a sharing mode.** Private (the default), shared, or tenant. `BusinessRecord.SharingMode` holds it; the units a shared record is listed for are rows of `kernel_data.record_shares`, keyed by the record type as its policy names it, the record id and the unit. The data library owns the table in its own context; every module context with business records maps it read-only, outside its migrations, so a list joins it in the same query and its paging counts stay right.
2. **Who sees a record.** A reader holding the type's read permission sees a record when their reach covers its owning unit, or a record-scoped grant names it, or it is shared with a unit their reach covers, or it is tenant-wide and they hold the read permission anywhere. A share is for reading only: every other action still needs its permission over the owning unit, and the policy's own filter is still joined with AND, so a policy narrows and never widens.
3. **Changing the sharing is a policy action.** The action is `share`, which a module's policy allows with a permission over the owning unit (usually its manage permission). Shared lists at least one active unit other than the record's own (`data.sharing.units_required`, `data.sharing.unit_not_active`); private and tenant list none. The change and `data.record_sharing_changed`, with the mode and the units before and after, are one transaction. `GET` and `PUT /api/v1/record-sharing/{type}/{id}` serve every record type: Authorization maps them for the data library, each needs a signed-in principal, and the record's policy decides the rest, an unreadable record being not found. The answer says whether the reader may change the sharing, decided without auditing a refusal. To a reader who may, it also lists the tenant's active units to choose from: whoever may share a record may see the names of the units they can share it with, though they may not otherwise read the tree.
4. **Reads outside the reader's home unit are audited.** A read by id of a private or shared record whose owning unit is outside the reader's home unit and the units below it writes `data.record_read`: the reader, the record, the owning unit, the action, and what allowed it (`scope`, `record`, `share` or `tenant`). A tenant-wide record is not audited: it is open to everyone on purpose. A refused read by id of a record that exists writes `data.record_read` with the outcome denied and the answer given (not found or forbidden), which closes A3's later item; an id of no record writes nothing, since there is nothing to protect and anyone could fill the log with guesses. A list writes one `data.records_listed` per page that holds such records, with their ids and the count, never one event per row.
5. **The working unit.** A shell session works for one unit: the home unit until the person chooses another, among their home unit and the units their own assignments name (with or without the units below them, and the root for a tenant-wide one), active ones only, in tree order. "All my units", the consolidated view, is offered when there is more than one unit to choose and a role reaches several units at once: a parent with children below it, or the whole tenant (TF-222 AC1). `POST /api/v1/me/working-unit` sets it, audited as `identity.working_unit_chosen`; `/me` answers the working unit and the choices; a choice the person may no longer make falls back to the home unit the next time `/me` is read. A token has no working unit and is refused one (`identity.working_unit.token`): it answers for its scopes.
6. **What follows the working unit.** New records take it as their owning unit (`NewRecords.OwningUnit`), unless the page chooses another. Settings resolve from it, for the signed-in person only (TF-222 AC3). Lists keep to it and the units below it, inside what the reader may see. In the consolidated view lists show everything the reader may see, and new records and settings follow the home unit. Reads by id are not narrowed, so a link to a record of another of the person's units still opens. Acting for someone is not narrowed either: the person works with the delegator's rights in full (ADR-0037). The header shows the choice only when there is more than one unit to choose, and not while acting: the unit's mark and name, and a menu on the tree.
7. **Reactivation.** A deactivated unit becomes active again under an active parent (`authz.unit.parent_inactive`), audited as `authz.unit_reactivated`; what it held never left it. A merged unit is never reactivated (`authz.unit.merged`).
8. **Readiness is supplied by modules.** `IUnitReadiness` in Authorization's Contracts gives a module's checklist for a unit: items in words, each done or not. The kernel supplies none. `GET .../organisation-units/{id}/readiness` answers the items and `ready` when every one is done, which is true when no module asks anything.
9. **Merging B into A.** A preview, which changes nothing, lists by module what would move: the business records B owns (`IUnitRecordOwners`, which every module with business records registers, deleted records included), the people homed at B (Identity), the assignments scoped to B, B's settings, the shares that list B, and B's child units. The merge moves all of it in one transaction under the tree's lock, and writes one `authz.unit_merged` listing every id moved (keys for settings) and what was dropped. Assignments move to A with or without the units below as before; one the person already holds at A with the same role and scope is dropped. A setting A has its own value of keeps A's; B's is dropped and listed as a conflict, and the history reads a reset at B and a set at A for each moved value. A record listed for both keeps one share. Each child moves as a move does, with its own audit event. B ends inactive with `merged_into_id` set to A. Refused: the root, A being B or a unit below it (`authz.unit.merge_into_own`), an inactive A, a child kind A cannot hold, a tree too deep after the move, and a unit merged already. It needs `authz.units.manage` at both units; the preview too, since it names people. Identity and Settings take part through `IUnitMergeParticipant` in Authorization's Contracts, which other modules that keep something at a unit implement the same way.
10. **A move keeps a unit's roles and settings.** Both are keyed by the unit's id, so they move with it; its own values stay and the rest come from its new parent. Splitting a unit by reassigning records is a later item.

## Options considered

- The shares in each module's own schema. Rejected: every module would carry the same table and code; one kernel table keyed by type and id serves every record, and every list joins it the same way.
- A share with a unit and the units below it. Deferred: a share lists units one by one; a share with children is a later item if products ask for it.
- A share that also lets the listed units change the record. Rejected: the owning unit stays answerable for its records.
- Auditing every read by id, or every row of a list. Rejected: reads inside one's own unit are the day's work, and a log of them would hide the reads TF-222 asks about.
- The working unit in the token, or as a header on each request. Rejected: a token answers for its scopes, not for a person at a desk; a header would let any call choose its unit silently.
- Starting every session in the consolidated view. Rejected: a new record needs one unit, and the home unit is what a person expects to start in.
- Narrowing reads by id to the working unit. Rejected: links between units would break, and the reader may see the record anyway.
- Merging as a background job. Rejected for now: a tenant's unit owns few enough records for one transaction, which is all or nothing. To revisit when a unit owns millions of records.
- B's settings overwriting A's. Rejected: the unit that stays keeps its own values; the preview names those that are dropped.

## Consequences

- Easier: a product's business records get sharing, the read audit, the working unit and merging through `BusinessRecord`, its policy, `NewRecords.OwningUnit` and one line of `AddUnitRecordOwners`; the shell's sharing control works on any record page.
- Harder: every list of business records joins the shares table (indexed by unit, and by type, record and unit). A merge moves assignments to A, so a person who held a role at B sees A's records too; the preview lists those assignments so the administrator decides with that in view.
- Later: a share with the units below; splitting a unit; merging as a job for very large units; access review campaigns that name a merged unit keep naming it.
